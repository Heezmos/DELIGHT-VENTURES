Free small business guide

Get online. Keep track of every enquiry.

A customer message can be lost even when the customer likes what you sell. Start with a clear place to find your business, one enquiry log, and a weekly review.

01 · Be findable

Show what you offer, where you work, and how customers can reach you.

02 · Record enquiries

Give each customer request an owner and a next action.

03 · Follow up

Review unanswered requests and the results every week.

Make it easy to contact you

Your online page should explain what you sell, whom you serve, where you operate, and how to contact you. Check it on a phone. Ask someone unfamiliar with the business to find the contact option and understand the offer.

Before paying for a website tool, check the domain, hosting, editing access, renewal cost, support, and how enquiries will reach your team.

Use one enquiry log

A spreadsheet is enough to begin. Record the date, customer name, contact channel, product or service, next action, owner, due date, and status. Avoid collecting sensitive information you do not need.

A useful log tells someone exactly what to do next. If a request has no owner or due date, it can still be forgotten.

Review it every week

Count new enquiries, unanswered enquiries, and sales. Follow up on promises. Notice the questions customers ask repeatedly, and improve the information you publish.

If a spreadsheet becomes difficult for your team to use, compare customer management tools against the process you already have. Check mobile access, permissions, data export, price, and local suitability before choosing one.

Your five-point checklist

  • Customers can find and contact the business from a phone.
  • Every enquiry has an owner and a next action.
  • Follow-up dates are recorded.
  • The team reviews unanswered requests weekly.
  • Tool costs and renewal terms are understood before purchase.
